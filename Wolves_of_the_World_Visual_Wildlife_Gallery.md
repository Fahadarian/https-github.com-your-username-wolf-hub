# 🐺 Wolves of the World — Visual Wildlife Gallery

> **Updated:** September 2026  
> **Purpose:** Educational wildlife reference and website content.  
> **Note:** Wild wolves are generally classified as species, subspecies, or populations rather than “breeds.”

---

## 1. Grey Wolf

**Scientific name:** *Canis lupus*  
**Type:** True wolf species  
**General status:** Globally widespread; conservation status varies by region.

### About

The grey wolf is the world's most widespread true wolf. It occurs across parts of North America, Europe and Asia and lives in habitats ranging from forests and mountains to tundra and grasslands.

### Diet

Mainly large and medium-sized mammals such as deer, elk, moose, caribou and smaller mammals.

### Main threats

- Habitat fragmentation
- Human-wildlife conflict
- Illegal killing
- Reduction of natural prey
- Roads and other infrastructure

---

## 2. Mexican Wolf

**Scientific name:** *Canis lupus baileyi*  
**Type:** Grey wolf subspecies  
**Status:** Highly endangered and intensively managed.

### About

The Mexican wolf is one of the most genetically distinctive grey-wolf subspecies. It historically ranged through parts of Mexico and the southwestern United States.

### Population

The U.S. Fish and Wildlife Service reported a **minimum wild population of 319 Mexican wolves at the end of 2025** in Arizona and New Mexico.

### Main threats

- Human-wolf conflict
- Illegal killing
- Vehicle collisions
- Habitat fragmentation
- Small-population genetic challenges

---

## 3. Arctic Wolf

**Scientific name:** *Canis lupus arctos*  
**Type:** Commonly recognized grey wolf subspecies  
**Habitat:** High Arctic regions.

### Adaptations

- Thick insulating fur
- Small ears relative to body size
- Strong feet adapted for snow
- High cold tolerance

### Diet

Depending on location, prey may include muskox, caribou, Arctic hare and other animals.

---

## 4. Indian Wolf

**Scientific name:** *Canis lupus pallipes*  
**Type:** Commonly recognized grey wolf subspecies/lineage  
**Habitat:** Grasslands, scrublands and semi-arid regions of the Indian subcontinent.

### Characteristics

Indian wolves are adapted to relatively warm and dry environments and are generally smaller than many northern grey-wolf populations.

### Main threats

- Habitat conversion
- Human-wolf conflict
- Declining prey
- Roads
- Fragmented habitat

---

## 5. Himalayan Wolf

**Scientific name:** Often treated as *Canis lupus chanco*  
**Type:** Taxonomically debated grey-wolf lineage/subspecies  
**Habitat:** High-altitude Himalayas and Tibetan Plateau region.

### Characteristics

It is adapted to cold, high-altitude environments and can live in some of the world's most extreme mountainous landscapes.

### Main threats

- Livestock conflict
- Declining wild prey
- Habitat pressure
- Human persecution
- Small and isolated populations

---

## 6. Iberian Wolf

**Scientific name:** *Canis lupus signatus*  
**Type:** Commonly recognized grey wolf subspecies  
**Region:** Iberian Peninsula, especially Spain and Portugal.

### Characteristics

Iberian wolves have distinctive darker markings and occupy forests, scrublands, mountains and agricultural landscapes.

### Conservation concerns

Regional populations experience different levels of protection and pressure, including human-wildlife conflict and habitat fragmentation.

---

## 7. Red Wolf

**Scientific name:** *Canis rufus*  
**Type:** Wolf species  
**Status:** Endangered; extremely rare in the wild.

### Current situation

The wild population in eastern North Carolina is extremely small and is supported by intensive conservation management. A much larger managed population is maintained in human care.

### Conservation methods

- Captive breeding
- Releases
- GPS/radio monitoring
- Cross-fostering
- Genetic management
- Habitat protection

---

## 8. Ethiopian Wolf

**Scientific name:** *Canis simensis*  
**Type:** Wolf species  
**Status:** Endangered.

### Population

Fewer than **500 mature individuals** are estimated to remain.

### Habitat

High-altitude Afroalpine ecosystems of Ethiopia.

### Diet

Primarily small rodents, especially highland rodents such as giant mole-rats.

### Main threats

- Habitat loss
- Agriculture
- Domestic-dog disease transmission
- Small isolated populations
- Climate and ecosystem changes

---

## 9. African Wolf

**Scientific name:** *Canis lupaster*  
**Type:** African *Canis* species  
**Region:** Parts of Africa.

### About

The African wolf was historically confused with the golden jackal. Genetic research demonstrated that African populations traditionally called golden jackals represent a distinct *Canis* lineage/species.

### Diet

May include:

- Small mammals
- Birds
- Reptiles
- Invertebrates
- Carrion

---

## 10. Maned Wolf

**Scientific name:** *Chrysocyon brachyurus*  
**Type:** Distinct South American canid — **not a true wolf**  
**Region:** Central and eastern South America.

### Characteristics

The maned wolf is famous for:

- Extremely long legs
- Large ears
- Reddish-orange coat
- Dark mane
- Omnivorous diet

### Diet

It eats fruit, small mammals, birds, reptiles and insects.

### Main threats

- Habitat conversion
- Road mortality
- Disease
- Fragmentation of grassland habitat

# Important Population Note

Population numbers are **date-sensitive estimates**. They can change because of births, deaths, migration, releases, new surveys and improved monitoring.

For a production wildlife website, display the **year/date of every population estimate** and identify the organization that produced the estimate.
